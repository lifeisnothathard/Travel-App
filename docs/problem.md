But there are **three here that I think are especially interesting to investigate further** — not as “winners,” but because they have very different product possibilities.

### ① The “Where the hell is the exit?” problem

Imagine you're in Tokyo.

Google Maps says:

> Shinjuku Station → 8 min walk

Technically true.

Reality:

**Shinjuku Station is basically a fucking underground city.**

Your app could answer:

> **Take Exit B12.**
> Walk toward the west concourse.
> Look for the blue sign.
> Take the escalator up.
> You'll emerge beside ___.

That's much more specific than another navigation app.

The product isn't **navigation**.

It's **last-500-metres navigation for confusing places.**

---

### ② The “I have 47 saved places” problem

Someone goes to Seoul.

They save:

* 18 restaurants
* 12 cafés
* 7 shops
* 6 attractions
* 4 photo spots

Then they're actually in Seoul thinking:

> “Okay... where the fuck do I go now?”

Your app could turn saved places into **contextual recommendations based on where they already are**.

For example:

> 📍 You're in Hongdae
>
> **Hungry?**
> 3 saved restaurants within 8 min
>
> ☕ Want somewhere quiet?
> 2 saved cafés
>
> 🛍️ Shopping?
> 4 saved stores
>
> 🌧️ It's raining
> → Here are your nearby indoor saves.

The key is that the app **doesn't create the itinerary for them**.

It helps them actually use the things they already wanted to visit.

---

### ③ The “something went wrong” travel app

This one is potentially REALLY interesting.

Instead of helping people plan the perfect holiday, the app helps when travel becomes messy.

Examples:

**Flight cancelled**

→ What do I do?

**Missed train**

→ What's my next option?

**Hotel says reservation doesn't exist**

→ What evidence do I need?

**Lost passport**

→ Who do I contact?

**Phone has no internet**

→ What information do I still have offline?

**Last train is gone**

→ How do I get back?

The product becomes something like a **travel emergency/offline decision companion**.

And this has a fascinating property:

People might install it **before every trip even if they hope they never need it.**

## One thing I'd change about your original goal

You said:

> “users who need it would definitely not delete it from their phone”

Don't make **retention** the first requirement.

Some extremely valuable travel apps are naturally **episodic**.

A person might only need it during travel, but think:

> “I'm going to Japan again. FUCK, I need that app.”

That's still a successful product.

What I'd really aim for is:

**“When this specific problem happens, I don't want to be without this app.”**

That's a much more achievable December MVP.

And **that** is the kind of project that could eventually give you a genuinely interesting portfolio story:

> *I identified a specific travel problem, researched users, designed a focused solution, built it, tested it with actual travellers, and iterated based on their behaviour.*

That's substantially more interesting than:

> *I built a travel app using Flutter.*
