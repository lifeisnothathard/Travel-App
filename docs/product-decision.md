Okay. If we're optimizing for **“crucial problem + underserved niche + realistically buildable by December”**, I would narrow it down pretty aggressively.

My strongest candidate for you is:

# 🧳 Travel disruption + “what do I do NOW?” for first-time independent travellers

Not a generic travel emergency app. Specifically:

> **A step-by-step recovery tool for travellers when their original plan suddenly breaks.**

Think about what happens when travel goes wrong:

* train cancelled
* missed connection
* last train already left
* hotel check-in problem
* phone has no internet
* suddenly stranded in an unfamiliar station
* attraction unexpectedly closed
* weather ruins an outdoor plan
* luggage storage unavailable
* payment/card doesn't work
* don't know whether to take taxi/train/bus
* don't know what information/documents they need

Google Maps can tell you **where things are**.

Trip planners can tell you **what you planned**.

Search engines can give you **20 different answers**.

But when you're standing in a foreign city thinking:

> **“Okay. My plan is fucked. What do I do next?”**

there isn't necessarily one obvious place to go.

---

## Why I like this niche

### 1. The problem is genuinely high-stakes

This isn't:

> “Hmm, which café should I visit?”

It's:

> **“I'm stranded somewhere I've never been and I need to make a decision.”**

That creates much stronger motivation to use the product.

### 2. It can be extremely specific

You don't need to solve *all* travel problems.

Your first version could literally focus on:

> **Missed/cancelled public transport while travelling independently in a foreign city.**

Then your user becomes:

**First-time international travellers aged ~18–35 who primarily use public transportation.**

That's already much more focused than:

> “People who travel.”

### 3. Offline functionality could become a major differentiator

This is the part I'd REALLY investigate.

Imagine the traveller has:

**No mobile data.**

They open the app and still have:

* emergency instructions
* saved hotel address
* important local numbers
* basic phrases
* their planned route
* offline recovery checklist
* previously downloaded transport information

That's an actual reason to keep the app installed.

# But there's an even more interesting niche 👀

If you want something **less crowded**, I'd investigate:

## 🚉 “Confusing transit” for first-time travellers

Not navigation.

**Transit confidence.**

Example:

You're Malaysian and visiting Tokyo for the first time.

Google Maps says:

> Shinjuku → Station X
> 18 min
> Transfer at Y

Technically, that's enough information.

But a first-time traveller might still wonder:

> Which platform?

> Which direction?

> Do I need to leave the station?

> Which exit?

> Why are there three different lines?

> Is this the correct train?

> What happens when I arrive?

Your product could specialize in turning complicated transit journeys into:

### **“Do this → then this → then this.”**

And eventually:

> **Platform 4**
> Take the train marked ___
> Get off after 3 stops
> Follow signs for ___
> **Use Exit 6**
> The landmark you'll see is ___

That is a much narrower product than Google Maps.

# Another niche I REALLY like for you

## 🕌 Muslim travellers navigating unfamiliar cities

Not another halal restaurant finder.

Instead:

> **“Can I realistically do my trip while maintaining my prayer routine?”**

Imagine planning a day:

**10:00** museum
**12:30** lunch
**1:15** travel
**2:00** attraction
**4:00** shopping

The app identifies:

> 🕌 Prayer window approaching
> 📍 Prayer space 6 minutes from your current route
> 🍜 Halal food nearby
> 🚶 Walking time
> 🚆 Transport time

The important part would be **route integration**, not simply displaying prayer times or halal restaurants.

There are already many Muslim travel products, so you'd need to research the exact gap carefully. But the underlying user need is very specific.

# If I were researching this with you

I wouldn't choose based on “this sounds cool.”

I'd score each idea against **five factual questions**:

| Question               | What we're looking for                                    |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pain**               | Does the problem genuinely cause frustration/loss/risk?   |
| **Frequency**          | Does it happen repeatedly during a trip?                  |
| **Existing solutions** | Are current apps genuinely solving it?                    |
| **Niche size**         | Enough users to matter, but specific enough to serve well |
| **MVP feasibility**    | Can *you* build a useful version by Dec 2026?             |

And there's one more:

### **“Would someone recommend this to another traveller?”**

That's the killer test.

Not:

> “Would you download this?”

but:

> **“If your friend told you they were going to Tokyo next month, would you tell them to download this?”**

That's the behaviour you're looking for.

### One thing I would NOT do

Don't start coding any of these yet.

You're giving yourself until **December**, so you actually have enough time to do this properly.

I'd spend the first **1–2 weeks** interviewing maybe **10–15 people** from the specific user groups, asking about their *last actual trip*, not:

> “Would you use my app?”

Because people are terrible at predicting what they'd use. 😂

Instead:

> “Tell me about the last time something went wrong while travelling.”

> “What did you do?”

> “Which apps did you open?”

> “What information were you looking for?”

> “What was the most frustrating part?”

> “Did you eventually solve it? How?”

Then we look for **the same pain appearing repeatedly**.

That gives you something much more valuable than an app idea:

**evidence that the problem actually exists.**

And THAT would give your December project the “good foundation” you're looking for.
